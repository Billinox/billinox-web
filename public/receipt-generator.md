## What Is a Receipt?

A receipt is a document provided by a business to confirm that money has been received from a customer.

A receipt commonly records:

- Who received the payment
- Who made the payment
- What was purchased or paid for
- The amount paid
- The date of payment
- The payment method
- A receipt or transaction reference

Receipts are useful for both businesses and customers because they provide a record of a completed payment.

## Receipt vs Invoice

An invoice and a receipt serve different purposes.

An invoice is generally a request for payment. It tells a customer how much they owe and when payment is expected.

A receipt is generally confirmation that payment has been received.

A typical billing workflow may therefore be:

1. A business provides a quotation or estimate.
2. The business issues an invoice.
3. The customer makes payment.
4. The business records the payment.
5. The business provides a receipt confirming payment.

Billinox provides tools and features supporting different parts of this billing workflow.

## Receipt vs Quotation

A quotation is used to communicate proposed pricing before a customer agrees to a purchase or service.

A receipt is used to confirm that payment has already been received.

For example:

- Quotation: "This service will cost ₦100,000."
- Invoice: "₦100,000 is due for the service provided."
- Receipt: "₦100,000 has been received."

The appropriate document depends on the stage of the customer transaction.
